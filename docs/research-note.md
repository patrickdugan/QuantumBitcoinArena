# Hash-based conditional disclosure for Bitcoin bridges: reproducing, stress-testing and costing Antichain Winternitz

Research prototype, 2026-10-20. Code, tests, fixtures and raw data are in this repository; every
number below is produced by a command in section 10 and stored in `results/`. Tables A to F are
generated by `scripts/summarize.py` (copy in `results/summary.md`).

## Bottom line

1. **The paper's headline holds.** I reproduced the reference crate's numbers and then re-derived
   them in an independent implementation, and I confirmed the sizes on Bitcoin Core 31.1 regtest:
   ACW (`k=4, L=16`, 20-byte digests) reveals a 1,024-bit input in **8,735 vB versus 18,499 vB** for
   per-bit Lamport, **-52.8%**. The byte-aligned `k=4, L=8` variant is **-47.8%**.
2. **Against what Mosaic does today it is not cheaper.** A Schnorr-adaptor reveal (one BIP-340 signature
   per 8-bit wide label, validated on Core with real signatures) costs **3,292 vB**, about
   2.7x less than ACW. ACW's case is that it is hash-only, post-quantum on the reveal step, and
   guarantees revelation inside the Script. Its case is not cost.
3. **I found no compression scheme that beats ACW at the same parameters, and I do not claim one.**
   Within the model where openers are forward-hashable, the admissible codewords must form an
   antichain, and ACW uses the largest one. Past `k=4` the on-chain gain flattens (-8% at `k=5`,
   -13% at `k=6`) while the off-chain table grows 14x and 211x. The template floor is about
   5.8 vB/bit.
4. **The script argument needs only preimage resistance, not collision resistance.** With 20-byte digests
   the evaluator-side cost is about `2^140` classically and `2^70`-`2^74` Grover iterations. The cap
   on the *whole* system's quantum margin is the 128-bit label length in ckt (about `2^64`), not the digests.
5. **This is not a post-quantum bridge.** Four things remain: a post-quantum replacement for Mosaic's
   elliptic-curve polynomial commitments (the hard one), a hash-preimage fault secret, a hash-based
   statement instead of Groth16, and an output type without a key path. Details in
   `docs/requirements-gap.md`.

## 4. Is there a stronger scheme? (compression)

**No scheme that I can defend beats ACW at the same `(k, L)`.**

*Optimality within the model.* Anyone holding the opener for coordinate `a` on a chain can compute the
opener for every smaller coordinate. So if two admissible codewords satisfy `c <= c'` coordinatewise, a
holder of the opening of `c'` can also open `c`, which is the equivocation/extra-label leak I demonstrate
in `key_reuse_equivocation_leaks_additional_labels`. The admissible set must therefore be an
antichain in the product order, and the largest antichain of `[0,L-1]^k` is the middle layer
(de Bruijn-Tengbergen-Kruyswijk), which is exactly ACW's constant-sum set. So `b <= floor(log2 N)`
for any design on `k` chains of length `L`. **[argued; the counts `N` are computed by DP and match
the reference sweep exactly.]**

*What is left is the script template.* From the generated scripts one chain costs
`50 + 9(log2 L - 1) + L` bytes (pk push, check, running sum, binary hash loop, opener, coordinate).
Per bit of capacity (`log2 L` bits per chain) that is minimised at `L = 16`-`32` at about 23.2 bytes, i.e.
**5.8 vB/bit as `k` goes to infinity**, which needs an unbounded table. Measured (`bench.json`,
`results/frontier.png`):

| k | L | bits/digit | capacity efficiency | vB/bit | table B/bit | vs `k=4, L=16` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 8 | 5 | 0.56 | 11.15 | 768 | small-table option (`k=2, L=16`: 11.31 at 256) |
| 4 | 8 | 8 | 0.67 | 9.34 | 5,504 | **byte-aligned**, 344 codewords for 256 values |
| **4** | **16** | 11 | 0.69 | **8.36** | 43,776 | reference point |
| 4 | 32 | 14 | 0.70 | 8.36 | 349,696 | no gain, 8x table |
| 5 | 16 | 15 | 0.75 | 7.68 | 628,480 | -8% on-chain, 14x table |
| 6 | 16 | 19 | 0.79 | 7.29 | 9,243,904 | -13% on-chain, 211x table |

What I considered and did **not** pursue (estimated, not built, so treat as estimates): posting the bits of each
coordinate instead of the count (saves about 2 bytes per bit-chunk but adds witness bytes; net about 1-2%);
mixed-radix packing across digits (breaks the per-digit label payload); non-power-of-two `L` (loses the MINIMALIF
range trick, needs explicit checks); and anything using `OP_CAT`, which is not enabled.
One real, small improvement: **dropping the bit item from Lamport** (built as `lamport-nobit`) saves one witness byte
per bit, 17.50 vs 17.75 vB/bit, which shrinks ACW's headline saving from 52.9% to 52.2% at `k=4, L=16`.

**Recommendation.** Use `k=4, L=8` when the table must stay small or the interface is byte-oriented (it matches the
8-bit wide label of ckt and Mosaic exactly): 9.4 vB/bit and 5.5 kB/bit of table. Use `k=4, L=16` when fees dominate
and 44 kB/bit of table is acceptable. Do not go beyond `k=4` without a reason.

## 5. Security and quantum parameters (summary of `docs/security.md`)

* Script argument: Lemma 1 (accepted set = antichain), Theorem 1 (guaranteed revelation, unconditional given an honest table),
  Theorems 2-3 (unforgeability and row privacy reduce to a backward step on a chain: a preimage problem on public values).
  Tagged `[exhaustive-tested]`/`[argued]`; nothing is mechanised.
* **Collision resistance is not used.** The garbler already knows all secrets; Bitcoin's single spend, not a hash,
  prevents two openings. Key reuse is catastrophic (Prop. 4), so each commitment is strictly one-time.
* Grover: 20-byte digests give about `2^70`-`2^75` evaluator-side iterations (including a `log2 L` chain term and the multi-target
  factor). That is above the `2^64` of AES-128 key search by iteration count. 32-byte digests give `2^118`-`2^122` for +26% on-chain.
* The **label length** (128 bits, AES-128 CCRND) caps the whole system near `2^64`. Hash commitments elsewhere should be 256-bit.

## 6. Adversarial testing

`crates/pqcd/tests/adversarial.rs` (20 tests) plus unit tests (14) and fixtures (2). All pass. They cover each requested class:

| Attack | Tests |
|---|---|
| Alternate-bit / alternate-value opening | Lamport both variants; ACW **every ordered pair** `c != c'` at `(2,8)`, `(3,4)`, `(3,8)`, `(4,8)` (118k pairs); script on all small pairs, a stride at `k=4` |
| Forward-hashing forgery | proptest (400 random strategies): accepted => decodes to the honest codeword; native == script on garbage |
| Unauthorized codeword substitution | **accept set equals the antichain exactly** for 5 `(k,L)` pairs over `[-1,L]^{k-1}` x last-opener indices; layers `R +- 1, 2` rejected |
| Malformed commitments / witnesses | `validate()` flags duplicate/short/mis-shaped terminals; missing, extra, short and swapped witness items; non-minimal numbers (consensus-valid, policy-invalid, **confirmed on Core**) |
| Replay and equivocation | cross-instance and cross-position replay rejected; **key reuse leaks** (quantified); defective commitments shown exploitable |
| Label permutation | table rows swapped (audit + value commitment catch it); digit order swapped; ckt-level: swapped wire labels, lied values, tampered rows |

I also **mutation-tested the suite**: dropping the last-chain `EQUALVERIFY`, shifting the sum target to `R+1`, and
removing the native range check each make between 2 and at least 8 of the 20 tests fail, and the restored code passes all 20. On Core, forged witnesses,
missing/extra/short items are rejected with the real error strings (`results/regtest.json`).

## 7. ckt integration

`pqcd-ckt` garbles a 16-input verifier (`valid(x) = [x == target]`: 16 XOR, 15 AND) with the real `ckt-gobble` engine, embeds a
fault secret through ckt's output translation, and commits each input byte with ACW `k=4, L=8` (344 codewords for 256 values, so
88 codewords alias onto lower values and no admissible opening is wasted). Each table row holds ckt's **wide label** for the byte,
the object `translate_input` consumes.

* **All 65,536 possible claims** evaluate correctly: output is `true` iff `x == target`; the secret is recovered iff the claim is invalid.
* The labels are derived **only after the script accepts**; all 343 other codewords of one byte, forged by forward hashing, are rejected by script and by label derivation.
* `demo regtest`: the garbler spends the committed leaf on Core, the evaluator reads the openers from the **confirmed
  transaction**, derives the labels and evaluates: valid claim -> output `true`, no secret; invalid claim -> output `false`, secret recovered.
* Golden vectors `fixtures/ckt_vectors.json` regenerate **byte-identically**.
* Not done: ckt's `.v5a` circuit-file path (its reader is built on `monoio`, which does not run on Windows); I used the
  `ckt-gobble` API directly. The circuit is a toy, not the Groth16 verifier.

## 8. What remains (summary of `docs/requirements-gap.md`)

Met: guaranteed revelation, no extra labels, authenticated input (given one-time keys and an honest table).
Not met: label **consistency across the garbled copies** (Mosaic's `vs3` commitments are secp256k1 and a hash-only
replacement loses the homomorphism), a **hash-preimage fault secret**, a **hash-based verified statement** (Groth16 is pairing-based),
a **Taproot-free output** (a CRQC can take the key path; P2WSH holds one ACW digit), **atomic reveal** above about 1.5K bits, and
**label length** (about `2^64`). Mosaic's paper page states no quantum analysis.

## 9. Does it improve a realistic bridge?

For a Mosaic-sized reveal (about 1,024-1,056 bits):

* **Versus Lamport: yes, materially.** 8.7 vs 18.5 kvB on Core (-53%), at the price of a 5-46 MB table (per-bit labels) or
  1.5-8.4 MB (one 32-byte secret per value), 2.7x the hash executions, and about 3x fewer witness items per bit (0.64 vs 2).
* **Versus today's adaptor reveal: no.** 8.7 kvB vs 3.3 kvB, i.e. +5.4 kvB per withdrawal, which is 54k / 270k / 540k sats extra at
  10 / 50 / 100 sat/vB.
* **What it buys for that price:** a reveal with no elliptic-curve step, and a Script-enforced guarantee that an accepted
  spend yields labels (the paper's stated weakness of adaptors; I did not independently analyse the adaptor scheme).
* **What it does not buy:** a post-quantum bridge (section 8).

So: a real improvement over Lamport, the right component if the design is committed to a hash-only reveal, and not
a cost improvement over the deployed approach.

## 10. Reproduce

Windows, Rust 1.99 MSVC toolchain, Bitcoin Core 31.1 (for the regtest steps).

## 11. Limits of this work

* Security statements are arguments plus tests, not machine-checked proofs, and they assume the hash functions behave generically.
* I did not run Mosaic (it needs FoundationDB) or audit it; section 8 rests on reading its specification and crates.
* Script execution times are from `bitcoin-scriptexec`, not Core; timings are single runs.
* 10K/100K table costs are extrapolated from sampled digits.
* The Schnorr baseline models one signature per 8-bit label with `OP_CODESEPARATOR`; Mosaic's real transaction layout may differ.
* Only the 1,024-bit cases were mined. The 10K/100K sizes were packed into standard-size transactions by weight but not broadcast.
* Atomicity across several transactions (100K bits) is a protocol problem I flag but do not solve.
